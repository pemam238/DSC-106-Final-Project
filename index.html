Definition

What is a drought?

A drought is a prolonged period of abnormally low precipitation relative to what a region typically receives. It's not about absolute dryness — it's about deficit.

When rain or snow falls well below historical averages for weeks, months, or years, soils dry out, rivers shrink, and water supplies dwindle — slowly and silently.

Surprise

Not just a desert problem.

We picture drought in cracked, sunbaked landscapes — but drought doesn't care about temperature. A rainforest, a snowy mountain range, even the Arctic tundra can run dry.

What matters is the gap between what falls and what's expected. Places that depend on reliable rain are often the most vulnerable when that rhythm breaks.
