Definition

What is a drought?

A drought is a prolonged period of abnormally low precipitation relative to what a region typically receives. It's not about absolute dryness, but deficit.

When rain or snow falls well below historical averages for weeks, months, or years, soils dry out, rivers shrink, and water supplies dwindle.

Surprise

Temperature is only part of the story.

Drought is not measured by heat alone. It happens when a place receives significantly less rain or snow than it normally expects.

That means drought can appear in many different climates; wherever the usual pattern of precipitation breaks!

01 / Africa & Oceania

Hot and dry

Sub-Saharan Africa and interior Australia combine intense heat with some of the lowest rainfall on the planet — the textbook drought zone.

02 / South America

The paradox

The Amazon is the wettest place on Earth, yet neighbouring northeast Brazil suffers chronic drought. Proximity is no guarantee of rain.

03 / Asia & North America

Hidden dryness

Central Asia and the American West sit far from ocean moisture. As temperatures rise, snowpack shrinks — turning seasonal dry spells into multi-year droughts.

04 / Europe

The new frontier

Southern Europe now regularly records drought conditions once considered rare. The Mediterranean basin is drying faster than almost anywhere else on the planet.
