Definition

What is a drought?

A drought is a prolonged period of abnormally low precipitation relative to what a region typically receives. It's not about absolute dryness, but deficit.

When rain or snow falls well below historical averages for weeks, months, or years, soils dry out, rivers shrink, and water supplies dwindle.

Surprise

Temperature is only part of the story.

Drought is not measured by heat alone. It happens when a place receives significantly less rain or snow than it normally expects.

That means drought can appear in many different climates; wherever the usual pattern of precipitation breaks!

The drought paradox

South America is both hot and wet. Oceania and Africa are hot but dry — drought risk grows where heat and low rainfall combine.

What is SPI?

The Standardized Precipitation Index measures how far actual rainfall deviates from the long-term average — in units of standard deviation.

Step 3

Mild drought begins

When SPI drops below −0.5, rainfall is noticeably below average. Fewer drops reach the ground. Soil moisture starts to fall.

Step 4

Severe drought

At −1.5 or below, drought is severe. Rain is rare. Rivers shrink. What was once a steady rhythm becomes near-silence.

Extreme drought
